  • An account is personal: one account per person. Do not share it with others.

4. Technical requirements and risks

You need a device with an up-to-date web browser with JavaScript and cookies enabled, internet access and an email address. Connecting your own AI assistant needs a client that supports the API or the MCP protocol.

Using services over the internet carries the usual risks, such as malware, a taken-over email inbox or phishing for sign-in details. The operator will never ask you for your password, an authentication code or an API token.

Virgil is not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat or prevent disease and gives no medical, psychological, therapeutic or financial advice. Charts, streaks, reference ranges and scores show only what you entered or imported.

The AI features (the morning briefing, the A.N.D.Y. daily plan, workout note parsing, experiment summaries, lab result import) produce content automatically and can be wrong: miss or misread a result, make something up, or suggest something that does not serve you. Check them before you rely on them. For health matters, consult a doctor; for mental health and addiction, a specialist; for finances, an adviser. 8. Availability and backups

Virgil runs on a single server. We make an effort to keep it available and secure, but we do not guarantee uninterrupted operation. The service may be unavailable because of maintenance, updates, failures of the server, the network or providers, or events outside our control.

The app makes backups: local copies of your account's database on the server (by default once a day, the latest 7 copies, settings in Settings > Data) and a daily encrypted copy of the whole instance off the server, kept for 30 days, with monthly copies kept for 365 days. The privacy policy has the details. Backups reduce the risk of data loss but do not rule it out; restoring from a backup may mean losing entries from the last hours or days.
